What is Living Soil Shortcut?

Living Soil Shortcut is BioFarmLab’s mobile app and practical course for gardeners, homesteaders and farmers who want to understand and improve their soil, prepare biologically active compost and apply that knowledge on their own land.

The package combines the complete 28-lesson course with Soil Radar, a Soil Regeneration Journal, Compost Calculator, Compost Log, saved history and Mike the Microbe, the in-app soil and compost assistant. The course is available both in the mobile app and on the learning platform. Editable spreadsheets and guides support work on a computer.

The aim is useful knowledge you can carry into your own growing: healthier plants, better-quality harvests and more of the fertility-building process under your own control. Understanding what your soil needs also helps you decide where your time, materials and money will be useful.

It is designed for people starting to explore soil biology and for experienced growers who want to connect their observations, composting methods and management decisions.

THE CONNECTION THAT MATTERS

From understanding soil biology to making your own decisions

A compost recipe gives you a set of ingredients. Understanding composting helps you work with the ingredients you actually have. A temperature reading records one moment. Understanding the process helps you interpret that reading alongside the pile’s age, moisture, materials and recent turning.

Living Soil Shortcut connects that understanding with practical tools. You can study the biological process, record what is happening on your land, plan a compost mix and follow its development. When a problem appears, Mike and the relevant lessons give you somewhere to work through it.

The course follows the relationships between plants, roots, fungi, bacteria and the organisms that feed on them. It explains how this soil food web participates in nutrient cycling and how management affects the conditions in which it operates. Composting is part of that larger picture: preparing and applying biological material with a purpose.

This broader understanding helps you connect jobs that are often treated separately. Choosing compost ingredients, observing water movement through soil and planning how to use a finished compost all contribute to the same growing system.

Understand the information. Know how to use it.

Each tool has a practical purpose: identify what needs attention, plan a mixture, recognise when to turn a pile or examine how your land is changing. The lessons explain what that information means and how to use it in your own growing.

HELP WHEN A QUESTION APPEARS

What does Mike the Microbe help you do?

Mike the Microbe is the soil and compost assistant inside Living Soil Shortcut. You can describe a problem in your own words and work through it using the knowledge behind the BioFarmLab programme.

You might be trying to understand why a compost pile has changed, how different observations fit together or which part of the course relates to your situation. Mike gives you a way to explore that question alongside the teaching and tools you already have.

The useful starting point is your actual situation: the materials in a pile, how long the process has been running, the readings you have recorded and what you have observed. That context makes the conversation relevant to your work. You can then return to the course for the explanation behind a suggested next step.

This combination is particularly useful when you understand the general method but meet a situation that differs from the example in a lesson. You can describe what changed, explain what you have already done and ask why a particular next step makes sense. The conversation helps you connect the general teaching with the situation in front of you.

For example, a question about a pile that has stopped heating can draw on the materials, its age and the readings you have kept. The Compost Troubleshooting lesson explains the underlying causes and checks. Mike gives you a place to explore your own question while you work with that knowledge.

THE PRACTICAL TOOLKIT

Keep observations, plans and compost records connected

The Tools menu groups together the parts of day-to-day soil and compost work. Soil Radar records assessments. The Soil Regeneration Journal keeps a record of work on the land. Compost Calculator helps you plan a pile, while Compost Log follows what happens during composting. History lets you return to saved records.

Each tool answers a useful question. Where does this plot need attention? How do my ingredients fit together? When should I turn this pile? What has changed since I began working on this land? The lessons explain the processes behind those questions, so the information can guide your work.

01 · OBSERVE YOUR SOIL

Soil Radar: bring five field observations into one view

Soil Radar records an assessment of a named plot or field and displays the observations on a radar chart. The five areas are succession, earthworms, aggregates, infiltration and compaction.

  • Succession: the vegetation present and what it can tell you about the stage of the growing system.
  • Earthworms: observations of earthworms in the assessed soil.
  • Aggregates: how the soil holds together and how stable its structure is.
  • Infiltration: how water enters the soil.
  • Compaction: the resistance and restrictions encountered in the soil.

The chart brings these observations together so you can see which aspects of the plot deserve closer attention. Infiltration concerns the movement of water into soil; compaction concerns restrictions within it. Looking at both helps you ask more useful questions about water, rooting conditions and the way the plot is managed.

In Simple Soil Tests, Filip explains the observations and how to carry out the checks. What Weeds Reveal About Soil and Biological Succession explain the information in the vegetation. The tools give you somewhere to record what you find; the lessons explain how to read it and connect it with your decisions.

A saved date and location make those observations useful later. Repeat the same checks on the same plot, return to the earlier assessment and examine which indicators have changed. You have a clearer basis for deciding what to investigate or work on next, and a starting point against which to review your work.

FROM THE FIELD TO THE TOOL

See the observations explained in a real lesson

In Simple Soil Tests, the connection is visible: Filip works on BioFarmLab’s land, then brings the observations together in Soil Radar and explains what the pattern suggests for that site. You see where the information comes from and how to start making sense of it.

The course provides the demonstration and interpretation. The app keeps the same learning useful when you return to your own plot. This link between explanation, observation and practical decisions runs through the soil and compost tools.

02 · RECORD YOUR WORK ON THE LAND

Soil Regeneration Journal: keep the story between assessments

A soil assessment records conditions on a particular date. The Soil Regeneration Journal gives you a place to record the work you carry out between assessments.

That history helps you interpret a later assessment. You can look back at when you applied compost, what you changed in the management of the plot and which observations followed. The question becomes specific: what was happening on this land between the starting assessment and the next one?

The lessons on compost application and the Living Soil, Step by Step module explain the purpose of the work you record. The journal keeps the sequence available for review. Together they help you plan deliberately, return to the reasoning behind an action and examine how the plot develops.

Used alongside Soil Radar, it connects an observation with a management decision, a record of the work and another observation later. That makes your own garden or farm a continuing source of useful learning across growing seasons.

03 · PLAN WITH THE MATERIALS YOU HAVE

Compost Calculator: turn available ingredients into a pile plan

Compost Calculator uses your planned pile dimensions and available materials to help you build a mixture. You can review the quantities and balance of the selected ingredients, adjust the plan and save it before starting the physical work.

That comparison gives you useful information before you build the pile. You can see how the materials you selected contribute to the planned mixture and how changing the quantities changes its balance. It helps you plan what to collect and combine while the recipe is still easy to adjust.

This helps you use resources already present in your garden or farm. You can work out a plan before collecting and moving a large amount of material, and retain the recipe when you start recording the composting process.

In Compost Ingredients, the materials are explained through their roles in the process. The thermophilic composting lessons show how the mixture is put to work at different scales. You learn to consider the food available to organisms, the physical structure of the mixture, moisture and air together. That is what makes the calculator’s proportions useful: you understand what you are trying to create and why each material belongs in the plan.

04 · KNOW WHEN TO TURN YOUR PILE

Compost Log: know when to turn your compost

Compost Log uses your recorded measurements and the pile’s progress to indicate when it is time to turn the compost. You record compost temperature, outside temperature, moisture and observations; the app brings those readings together with the history of the process to help you act at the right time.

A temperature that is falling after an active period means something different from a fresh mixture that has barely warmed. The sequence of readings helps you see which story you are looking at. Notes about the pile add the context needed to interpret the change.

The thermophilic composting lessons explain the biological process behind the temperature changes, why turning matters and how to manage the active phase. Compost Troubleshooting explains what to investigate when the process develops differently from what you expected. The app’s turning guidance gives you a practical signal; the teaching helps you understand why that signal matters and how to act on it.

You can then follow what happens after the turn and return to the history when planning your next batch. The information helps you manage the pile while it is active and understand the process more clearly as you gain experience.

History: return to previous records

History provides access to saved assessments, piles and readings. It gives a later observation something concrete to be compared with: the previous condition of the same plot, or the recipe and development of an earlier compost batch.

When you revisit a lesson, you can relate its explanation to records from your own work. You can examine what you changed, what you observed afterwards and which question to investigate next. The value of the records grows as you become better at interpreting them.

Questions about the Living Soil Shortcut app and course

Is Living Soil Shortcut a course or an app?

It is a package containing both. The complete 28-lesson course is available inside the mobile app and on the learning platform. The package also includes soil and compost tools, Mike the Microbe, editable spreadsheets and guides.

Does the app include the whole course?

Yes. All 28 lessons are included in the app. They cover living soil foundations, reading soil, compost methods, common recommendations and practical regeneration.

Who is it for?

Gardeners, homesteaders and farmers. Beginners can follow the course in sequence; experienced growers can revisit subjects, organise records and use the tools alongside their existing work.

Does it cover compost extracts and compost teas?

Yes. Both have dedicated lessons within the Building Soil Biology module, alongside thermophilic composting, vermicomposting, the Johnson–Su bioreactor and compost application.

Can it help me understand a compost problem?

The package includes a compost troubleshooting lesson, Compost Log for the pile’s history and Mike the Microbe for exploring soil and compost questions.

Can I use it on a computer?

Yes. The full course is also available on the learning platform, with editable Google Sheets and downloadable guides for desktop use. The same purchase includes mobile and computer-based access.

Is it available to Australian growers?
