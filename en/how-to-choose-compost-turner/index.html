A compost turner: turning hours of work into minutes

Why use a compost turner? A machine that makes turning large windrows, composting horse manure and producing compost on the farm easier.

BioFarmLab·22/10/2026·updated 30/10/2026
Italiano →Français →Español →Polski →Deutsch →Čeština →

Turning a large compost windrow with a fork can take hours. A compost turner does the work as the tractor travels along the windrow: it loosens, mixes and aerates the material. Work that would take hours by hand takes minutes with a turner. The exact time depends on the size of the windrow and the machine.

That makes a huge difference when you have more than a few wheelbarrows of plant residues or manure to process. Composting becomes a routine you can repeat on the farm, without the heavy burden of manual turning.

Why turn compost?

Good compost is made by living organisms that break down organic matter. Many of them need oxygen. When a wet windrow compacts into a dense mass, air stops reaching its centre. The material starts to putrefy, unpleasant smells develop and valuable nutrients are lost.

Turning loosens the material, lets air in and mixes wetter and drier parts. A turner does all this in one pass. That makes aerobic composting much easier to manage, even with several large windrows.

The machine does not replace knowing how to choose materials, manage moisture or decide when to turn. It removes one of the biggest obstacles: the time and effort needed to manage large amounts of compost regularly.

A stable has more than a manure problem

Every day, more droppings and bedding leave the stable. The growing heap in the manure storage area takes up space and needs dealing with. When the material is wet and compacted, oxygen runs short inside it. Putrefaction, strong odours and the loss of nutrients that could later feed plants follow.

Storing manure is not the same as managing a composting process. Horse manure and bedding are valuable raw materials. With proper management, they can become compost that helps restore soil fertility, instead of an unwieldy heap that accumulates for months.

Our four-part horse-manure composting series (in Polish) shows the strong smell subsiding after the first turn. After 21 days, the material smells like forest litter. Microscopy also reveals organisms developing within it. After active composting, the material continues to mature.

For a stable owner, this changes the way manure is seen: a disposal problem becomes a valuable resource. A turner makes regular composting possible without hours of manual work on every windrow.

Another tool for the regenerative farm

Farms have machines for sowing, harvesting and spreading materials. A compost turner has a different job: it helps produce compost, which supplies organic matter and, when properly prepared, beneficial organisms to the soil.

It is useful beyond stables: livestock farms, market gardens, orchards and compost producers handling larger volumes can all use it. Wherever windrows need regular attention, fast mixing saves work and makes it easier to increase production.

Finished, properly prepared compost can be applied to soil. It can also be used to make compost extracts and teas. The turner does not make these liquids. It helps prepare the biologically valuable compost on which the next steps depend.

Watch a compost turner at work

The machine is easiest to understand in action: a tractor moves it along the windrow while the rotor lifts and mixes the material. What would require prolonged manual turning happens during a single pass.

Choosing the size

The choice depends mainly on windrow dimensions, the amount of material and your tractor.

The 10 kW compost turner is designed for farm-scale work. It handles windrows up to 170 cm wide, works with tractors from around 20 hp and has an integrated watering system.

The 40 kW compost turner is intended for larger windrows and greater volumes. It has a gate 300 cm wide and 160 cm high, its own water pump and a 1,000-litre tank. Those dimensions describe the machine’s gate; they do not automatically define the permissible size of every windrow.

You do not have to work out every technical detail yourself. Information about your tractor, material and planned workload is enough to start choosing a suitable machine.
